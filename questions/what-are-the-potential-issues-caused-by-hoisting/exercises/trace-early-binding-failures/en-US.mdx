What happens when this script runs?

```js
console.log(total);
console.log(status);

var total = 4;
let status = 'ready';
```

## Options

### undefined-then-reference-error (correct)

It logs `undefined`, then throws `ReferenceError`.

> The `var` binding is initialized to `undefined` during setup, while the lexical binding remains uninitialized in its temporal dead zone.

### both-undefined

It logs `undefined` twice.

> `let` does not receive an early `undefined` value; reading it before initialization throws.

### four-ready

It logs `4`, then `ready`.

> Initializer expressions still execute at their source positions after both logs.

### first-reference-error

The first log throws `ReferenceError`.

> The `var total` binding already exists and is readable as `undefined`.
