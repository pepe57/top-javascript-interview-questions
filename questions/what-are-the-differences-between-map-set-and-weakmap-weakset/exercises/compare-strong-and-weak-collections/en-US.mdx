Which statements about strong and weak collections are correct? Select all that apply.

## Options

### map-any-key (correct)

A `Map` accepts values of any JavaScript type as keys and is iterable in insertion order.

> It is a general-purpose strong key-value collection.

### weak-map-object-keys (correct)

A `WeakMap` accepts objects and non-registered symbols as keys without keeping those keys alive by itself.

> Entries follow key reachability, making the collection useful for attached metadata.

### weak-collections-not-iterable (correct)

`WeakMap` and `WeakSet` intentionally do not expose enumeration or size.

> Observable enumeration would conflict with nondeterministic garbage-collection timing.

### weak-set-membership (correct)

A `WeakSet` is useful when recording object membership should not control object lifetime.

> Its entries are held weakly rather than strongly retained by the set.

### weak-values

A `WeakMap` weakly holds arbitrary primitive keys and automatically weakens all of its values independently.

> Keys have restricted weak identity semantics; values remain associated as long as their key is reachable.
