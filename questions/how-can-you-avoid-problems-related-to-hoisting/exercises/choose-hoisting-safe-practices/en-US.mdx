Which practices reduce bugs related to declaration timing and early access? Select all that apply.

## Options

### const-default (correct)

Use `const` by default and `let` only when reassignment is required.

> Lexical declarations avoid `var`’s early `undefined` reads and make mutation intent explicit.

### declare-before-use (correct)

Keep declarations close to and before their first use.

> Source order then matches the reader’s mental execution order and avoids temporal dead zone access.

### static-analysis (correct)

Enable rules such as `no-use-before-define` and `no-undef`.

> Static checks catch risky references before runtime.

### let-not-hoisted

Rely on the fact that `let` and `const` declarations are not instantiated until their source line.

> Their bindings exist from block setup but remain uninitialized in the temporal dead zone.

### var-everywhere

Use `var` everywhere so early reads consistently return `undefined` instead of revealing mistakes.

> Silently reading `undefined` can hide ordering bugs; lexical declarations and explicit ordering are safer.
